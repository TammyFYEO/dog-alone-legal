Dog Alone

Privacy Policy

Last updated: September 7, 2026

Overview

Dog Alone helps a dog owner notice when they leave a saved Dog Location and, when needed, notify trusted Circle members with transactional safety messages. Messages are used for Circle invitations and dog-safety incident alerts, not advertising or promotional SMS.

Information Dog Alone Handles

The current app and backend may handle account identifiers, household records, Circle member names and mobile phone numbers, dog names, saved Dog Location coordinates on the device, session and incident status, responder assignments, secure invitation and responder tokens, and SMS delivery metadata.

Dog Alone uses iOS location permission to save a Dog Location and recognize when the owner leaves or returns. The app stores the saved Dog Location coordinates in local device storage. Dog Alone also uses Apple notification permission for local owner notifications.

Circle Contacts and SMS

Dog Alone users add trusted contacts to their Dog Circle by entering a contact name and mobile phone number. A contact receives an invitation explaining that they have been invited to join the user's Dog Alone Circle. The contact must accept before becoming an active Circle member and receiving future dog-safety incident alerts.

SMS message frequency varies based on Circle invitations and Dog Alone incidents. Message and data rates may apply. Recipients can opt out of SMS messages by replying STOP.

Dog Alone does not sell mobile phone numbers and does not share mobile phone numbers with third parties for marketing or promotional purposes.

Responder Links

Invitation and incident messages include secure links. The responder web page uses the token from the link to call limited backend functions for invitation status, invitation accept or decline, incident status, incident accept or decline, arrival confirmation, and assignment release when available.

The responder web page is designed not to store raw tokens in browser storage, not to use analytics, and not to use cookies. Before a responder accepts an incident, the page shows only minimal context permitted by the backend.

Service Providers

Dog Alone uses Supabase for backend authentication, database storage, server-side functions, and public responder pages. Dog Alone uses Apple services for iOS location and local notification permissions. Dog Alone is prepared to use Twilio for transactional SMS delivery when SMS is enabled.

Security and Retention

Secure invitation and responder tokens are stored by the backend as hashes. SMS delivery records are designed to store delivery status and provider message identifiers, not raw tokens, full secure links, SMS message bodies, or phone numbers.

Dog Alone keeps information for as long as needed to provide and operate the service unless it is deleted or replaced through the app or backend operations. The current repository does not define a fixed automatic deletion schedule.

Your Choices

Users can manage iOS location and notification permissions in device settings. Dog Alone users can add, update, pause, or remove Circle members in the app. Circle invitation recipients can accept or decline an invitation, and SMS recipients can reply STOP to opt out of SMS messages.

Contact

For privacy or SMS questions, contact the Dog Alone operator through the contact method provided with the app distribution or campaign registration.
